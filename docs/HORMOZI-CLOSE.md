# Hormozi on closing: what to take into the Visualize call

Source: "How to Sell Better than 99% Of People (4 HOUR ULTIMATE GUIDE)", Alex Hormozi, YouTube JE2_7elAcxM (4:43:18). Seven stitched talks built for high ticket phone sales and sales teams. The team management third is marked skip.

## 1. The close framework

Only three things come out of your mouth on a call: questions, restatements, short stories. Never a feature list. The pitch itself stays under three minutes. The script is the CLOSER acronym (2:44 and 4:15).

**C. Clarify why they are here.** "What made you reach out?", "What's your goal right now?", "Why is that important to you?". If they say they just want information, do not accept it: nobody takes calls all day collecting information, there is a problem, what is it.

**L. Label the problem.** "So what I'm hearing is you're struggling with X, does that sound about right?" They have to say yes and own it. You cannot cure what they will not admit they have.

**O. Overview past pain.** The pain cycle, four questions on repeat: what have you tried, how long did you do it, how long ago, how did it go, what else. Keep cycling until they run out. Then: it is not your fault, you were missing one piece, and only then "want to hear how I'd solve that?" Their exact words are the ammo for the close.

**S. Sell the vacation, not the plane flight.** Three pillars, never more. Each pillar gets a 30 second story instead of a feature. Example: "do you need motivation to watch your favorite show? No, because you look forward to it." Everyone gets Maui; the packages only change how fast and comfortable the trip is.

**E. Explain away concerns.** Price objections mean you did not build value. Decision maker objections mean you missed something earlier. Stalls mean they do not know how to decide. Handle it, then ask again. Looping is unlimited as long as you resolved something before each ask.

**R. Reinforce.** Customers decide in the first 48 hours after paying whether they trust you. Personal video or voice memo, warm onboarding.

Memorize two things only: the pillar stories and the objection responses. Everything else is questions, so if you get lost you ask the next one.

From the other talks:

- **Three beliefs before the ask** (0:18): it gets them the goal the way they want; people around them will support it; it works for them specifically.
- **Obstacles before price, objections after** (0:16, 1:43). Raise partner, time and fit before the number. "Kill zombies while they are far away."
- **The gasp** (0:03): no gasp at the price means you went too low.
- **Prep** (1:30): ten percent of the call length in research.
- **Book a meeting from a meeting** (1:24): never hang up without the next time set.
- **Ask, then ask again** (1:54): sales are proportional to asks.
- **Tone** (2:19, 4:28): words are ten percent, delivery ninety. Conviction fixes tone.

## 2. Objection handling

Every objection hands power to something outside the person (0:20). Three layers, outside in: circumstances (time, money, fit), other people (partner), self (stalling). Peel one and the next shows. Never argue. Posture is childlike curiosity, head tilted: "huh, tell me more about that." Win the argument, lose the sale.

**Time (0:26)**
- Busy season: do you want this to last? Will you be busy again? Then start while busy so it sticks.
- No hours: a good program removes what is not working, it does not add to the pile.
- "When I have time": a sequence fallacy. The thing you are putting off is what gives you the time.

**Money (0:32)**
- "Is this a lot of money to you?" Yes? Good, that is why you will take it seriously.
- "If all this did was X, is it worth it?" Yes? Then price is not the issue, belief is. Talk about belief.
- Anchor against the alternative (a degree, years of trial and error).
- You will pay either way, in money or in time.
- Resourceful not resources: everyone has found money for a surprise bill.
- The Ferrari line: if I sold you a Ferrari for five grand you would find the money.

**Fit (0:40)**
- New identity, new priorities. Change the change: what you have been doing got you here.
- Pain of staying the same versus pain of changing.
- The hypothetical: "if this were perfect, would you do it?" No means a trust problem, back up. Yes means "what's the gap between perfect and this?" Most cannot name one, so the issue is them.

**Partner (0:48, 2:56, 4:23)**
- "What if they said no?" One in three say they would do it anyway. Sign them.
- "What do you think they'd object to?" Handle that; the partner is not on the call.
- Past agreements: do they know you are struggling with this? Do they approve? Then why would they be against fixing something they already dislike?
- Support not permission. Rules reversed: if they needed this for their dream, would you back them?
- Delayed close: card down, charge Friday, if the partner really says no I tear it up.
- Prevention: ask at booking whether anyone else is part of the decision and get them on the call.

**"I need to think about it" (0:54)**
- First, always: "Totally. What's the main thing you're weighing?" Think about it together.
- Past: this is not a fast decision, you have wanted this for years. Do not let a bad past purchase burn you twice. Tired of another year of almost?
- Present: the rocking chair. You will not sit and think, life takes over and you land back here. Decisions need information, not time, and I am the information. Then teach the decision: do you believe this gets you closer, do you trust me to deliver, do you believe it works for you. Three yeses leaves only access to funds.
- Future: play it out five years. The options: do it and win, do it and the guarantee covers you, do nothing and stay here. Which risk free option do you want.
- Fear: "what are you most afraid of happening if you buy?" then "let's play it out, best case, worst case." He closed more on this than any other line.
- Bonus: the reason they give for not doing it is the reason to do it.

## 3. Price, anchoring, plans, honest urgency

- **Highest number first** (3:31). State the full plan total, then "prepay and save 20 percent, half down saves 10." Never show the low number and add fees for plans. Say "prepayment discount", never "paid in full"; the discount is their benefit.
- **Step downs** (3:33): prepay, financing, half down, straight plan, then just book the next visit. Everyone buys something.
- **Price tied to the gap** (3:17): current state, desired state, obstacle, then the price is the cost of closing the gap. They named the goal, so the price came from them.
- **Card on file during setup** (3:14) so the close is "use the card on file?"
- **Plan into recurring** (3:29): a program sold as a plan that rolls into the same monthly amount keeps people far longer than month to month.
- **Upsell sizing** (3:26): about five times the base price; 20 percent take it and average ticket doubles.
- **Honest urgency**: he names false urgency and scarcity as reasons prospects stop showing up (3:57). Instead: same or next day appointments, guarantees and trials ("make an informed decision from the inside"), prepay discounts, and the cost of inaction. The guarantee is what makes "every option is risk free except doing nothing" true.

## 4. For Visualize, in Rob's voice

The 15 minute concept call is CLOSER with a three minute pitch and the rest in C, L and O.

**Clarify and label.** "So what made you say yes to a call? ... Got it. The work's good but the brand doesn't match it. That sound right?"

**Pain cycle.** "Have you tried fixing this before? Who did it? How'd that go? What else?" Then: "Honestly that's not on you. You were missing the piece that ties it together. Want me to show you what I'd do?"

**Three pillars: look, trust, leads.** Not Wix, not pages, not revisions. "A brand that looks like a real shop, a site people trust on their phone, and a Google listing that sends new customers." One real client story per pillar. The concepts are the vacation photos.

**Price tied to the gap.** "You're here, you want to be here. The gap is a logo people remember, a site that works on a phone, and a Google listing that's set up. That's Web Essentials, $500."

**Highest number first.** "Launch Plan is $1,200. Most people do $200 a month for six months." A prepay discount is Hormozi's move but conflicts with hard rule 3 (never discount by cutting price), so that is Rob's call. Removing scope stays the lever.

**Step downs.** Build Plan, Launch Plan, one package at $750 or less, Social Refresh at $150, then "let's at least book a 30 minute brand review next week." Nobody leaves without a next step.

**Objections.**
- Money: "Is $500 a lot right now? Good, that's why it'll get used. If all it did was bring three new customers a month, worth it? Then it's not the price, it's whether you believe I'll deliver. Let's talk about that."
- Time: "You're busy now and you'll be busy in spring. I build it. You give me 30 minutes on the form and two rounds of feedback."
- Partner: "What do you think they'd say no to? ... Do they like how the shop looks online right now? Then why would they be against fixing it?" Put "anyone else part of this decision?" in the booking text so it never shows up at the close, and use the two times to pick for a call with both.
- Think about it: "For sure. What's the main thing you're weighing?" Then: "Do you think this gets you closer? Do you trust me to do what I say? Will it work for your shop? Then let's start." Two revision rounds plus the concept already in their hands is the risk reversal.
- Bad past experience: "Don't let the last guy burn you twice."

**Honest urgency.** Real start slots ("I take two builds a month, next open start is the 10th"), the deposit locking the price, and the cost of waiting ("another quarter of people finding your competitor on Google").

**Reinforce.** Within an hour of the deposit: voice memo from Rob, onboarding email, form link. That window decides whether they become a retainer client.

**Referrals at delivery.** "Who do you know that's as serious about their shop as you?"

**Skip** (high ticket or team only): comp plans, sales directors, setters and closers, 20 consults a day, outsourced teams, 36 month financing, Gong analytics, cutting reps in 14 days, the "ghetto tone" and Cheetos husband bits. Rob's tone is lighter and should stay that way.

## 5. CRM feature ideas

1. **Guided call stepper**: CLOSER steps in the call console, each with the question and an answer field. Supports C, L, O.
2. **Say it back box**: composes "So what I'm hearing is..." from the goal and pain fields for Rob to read aloud. Supports L.
3. **Pain cycle rows**: repeating tried / how long / result rows, nudging until two are filled. Supports O.
4. **Pillar story cards**: look, trust, leads, each with a 30 second client story and the matching concept image. Supports S.
5. **Objection cards**: drawer keyed by time, money, fit, partner, stall, with Rob's lines and the logic in one line. Supports E.
6. **Loop counter**: "asked for the sale" button logging each ask and the concern resolved before it. Supports E.
7. **Pricing presenter**: pick a package or plan, shows full price first, then monthly, then the step down ladder. Supports price presentation.
8. **Gap builder**: current, desired, obstacle fields that render the diagnostic sentence and suggested package. Supports price tied to goal.
9. **Partner check at booking**: "anyone else part of this decision?" captured at booking, flagged on the call card. Kills the authority objection early.
10. **Three question close checklist**: gets closer, trusts me, works for you, funds, ticked before the deposit link unlocks. Supports the stall close.
11. **Next step required**: a call record cannot close without a booked meeting or a deposit. Supports book a meeting from a meeting.
12. **48 hour reinforce tasks**: deposit paid creates voice memo, onboarding email and form check tasks. Supports R.
13. **Follow up cadence**: no decision calls get day 1, 3 and 7 tasks with the saved main concern pre filled. Supports E over time.
14. **Concept delivery tracking**: built, sent, opened, shown on call, per lead. Supports S and the free concept hook.
15. **Pre call prep card**: Google listing, Instagram, site, reviews, one personal detail, shown before dialing. Supports prep and trust.
16. **Funnel stats**: booked, showed, offered, closed, cash collected, weekly. A 20 percent lift in show rate equals a 20 percent lift in close rate.
